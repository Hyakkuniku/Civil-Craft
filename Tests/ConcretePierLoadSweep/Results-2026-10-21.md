# Concrete Pier tall/heavy gameplay test — 2026-10-21

## Conclusion

Keep the current strength **provisional and unchanged**. The tested material supports useful 10–20 tonne gameplay loads with a suitable frame, but a tall column carrying most of a large load still overloads. These tests do not justify a strength increase or establish final balance for every contract.

No live material, contract, scene, player-save, account, or website values were changed. Only diagnostic code and reports under `Tests` were added/extended. Before/after source hashes matched. No Unity Refresh is required for these tests.

## Current material

- Tension: 400,000 N; base compression: 2,100,000 N.
- Single placed member: 240 kg/m and 900 cost/m; maximum length 30 m.
- Height weakening: reference 10 m, exponent 2, minimum multiplier 0.15.
- Effective compression: 336,000 N at 25 m; 315,000 N at 26–30 m. The minimum-strength floor begins at approximately 25.82 m.

These are game-model presets, not engineering ratings.

## Test A: isolated supported pier

The actual production solver and material methods were tested at 10, 20, and every integer height from 25 to 30 m against 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 40 tonne vehicle point loads: 56 height/load cases. Each case repeated sample-for-sample. The fixture uses two deliberately massless/non-limiting deck members, fixed banks and one central pier; the real pier's self-weight remains active. It isolates column behavior and does not include a real frame's weight or load sharing.

## Test B: the saved curved bridge's frame

The current saved Marsh bridge contained the original 35 road/beam members but no piers. Two current-material piers were added **only in test memory** at the original shared roadway nodes 3 and 9, at X 1276.8062 and 1323.3812, starting at foundation Y -10. At the original heights, this reproduced the previous 23.3% whole-bridge and 14.6% road/beam peaks at 3 tonnes.

Only the two exclusive footing nodes were then lowered by a common offset. All roadway/beam positions, curvature, original anchors and shared connectivity stayed unchanged. This represents a hypothetical deeper foundation, not the current site's placement settings. The right pier stays approximately 1.0525 m shorter than the left: the tested pairs range from 25/23.9475 m to 30/28.9475 m.

Six heights and seven loads give 42 height/load pairs. Each pair compares actual pier mass, omitted piers, and zero-mass piers in memory: 126 comparisons, each repeated exactly. All three configurations were structurally stable under the solver's matrix-rank check.

At 20 tonnes, the frame without piers reaches 62.2%; with piers the whole-bridge peak is 54.4–57.9%. At 30 tonnes, the no-pier frame reaches 106.6%, compared with 70.0–74.5% with piers. At 40 tonnes, roadway tension, not pier compression, becomes the governing member; the supported frame reaches 91.5–93.8% versus 151.3% without piers.

Slightly lower global stress at some greater heights does **not** mean taller columns are inherently stronger. The minimum compression floor, changing axial stiffness and the frame's redistribution of load interact. These are whole-frame maxima and may move between members and vehicle positions.

## Balance recommendation and remaining checks

Do not increase the strength solely to make the overall stress number lower. Current tests retain useful headroom at 10–20 tonnes, make 30 tonnes a more demanding design problem, and expose overload where one tall pier must take most of the load. The curved frame's stronger performance comes from load sharing, not invulnerable material.

Only Marsh currently explicitly allows Concrete Pier, with a nominal 3,000 kg test load. Ford, Brooks and Ronan still have user-deferred load/budget values and do not currently opt in this material. Final balance must be checked again against their actual intended loads and geometry if Concrete Pier is later allowed there; no contracts were enabled or adjusted in this task.

These are repeatable tests of the production 2D axial-truss stress model, not native PhysX crossing, Android/device, terrain-contact, affordability, cost-star or real-world engineering certification. Calculated rows above 100% describe the pre-break model; runtime failure/redistribution was not replayed. Keep the preset and playtest the final intended heavy-load sites before shipping their balance.
