Civil Craft: Bridge Edition
One-sentence answer: Civil Craft is an Android-based 3D bridge construction game that supports
basic structural mechanics learning through guided contracts, material and budget decisions, load
tests, lesson review, and quizzes.

03 / THE TECHNICAL ANSWER
How your stress calculation works
1. Convert the design into a structural model
The game reads node positions, connections, member lengths and angles, material properties, and fixed
supports. The current solver is a deterministic, quasi-static 2D truss model. It primarily calculates axial tension
and compression.
2. Apply dead and live loads
Dead load: Member mass is based on placed length and mass per metre. Its weight is shared between its
endpoints.
Live load: The supported vehicle/cargo mass is applied along the road based on its position. Multiple-load tests
combine supported vehicle contributions.
Conversion: Force in newtons = mass in kilograms x 9.81.
3. Solve member forces
The solver assembles a stiffness matrix and solves K u = F: K represents the connected structure, u represents
calculated node displacements, and F represents applied loads. It then obtains each member's axial force. The
stiffness values are simplified gameplay values related to configured capacity, rather than measured material
elasticity and section properties.
Member utilization (%) = |axial force| / applicable force capacity x 100
Example: 6,000 N / 10,000 N x 100 = 60%.
Use the tension limit for pulling and the compression limit for pushing.
Ropes act in tension only. Configured pier compression capacity reduces with increased height. In normal
capacity-based failure, a ratio of 1.0 (100%) or more can trigger member breakage. Deformation and impacts
provide additional failure or warning behavior.
What the numbers mean
Stress percentage: Force-to-capacity utilization, not an MPa measurement. Engineering axial stress is sigma
= force / cross-sectional area.
Peak stress: The recorded highest tracked stress value, rather than an average across the entire bridge.
Estimated safety: Estimated single-load capacity / specified live-load mass. The scalar estimate is hidden for
multiple-vehicle tests.
Keep the limit clear: The model does not independently calculate a full 3D bending, shear and torsion response. PhysX movement
and lesson coverage should not be described as proof of full engineering analysis.

04 / QUESTIONS 1-7
Design choices and scope
01. Why can players not edit bridge width?
Width is a predefined condition of each task. Our builder and solver work in a 2D plane, so players
focus on member placement, supports, materials and cost. Editable width would require explicit
modeling of transverse load distribution, deck behavior and torsion. It is a possible future extension.
02. Does width affect a real bridge's strength?
Yes. Width can affect self-weight, load distribution, stiffness and torsional behavior. We acknowledge
those effects, but our current design does not expose width as an independent variable. We should
describe width as fixed within a simplified model, rather than claim that width has no effect.
03. Why call the game 3D when the analysis is 2D?
The environment, characters, vehicles and bridge presentation are three-dimensional. Bridge drawing
and structural analysis occur on a two-dimensional plane. These are different aspects of the system,
and we state both to explain exactly what the game represents.
04. Why use a simplified truss model?
It provides a manageable way to connect geometry, supports and loads with axial member demand. It
supports the game's intended conceptual tasks within the development scope. It also has limitations for
beam bending and full 3D behavior, which we disclose rather than treating it as a universal bridge
model.
05. Are beam, truss, arch and suspension bridges equally realistic?
They are covered as structural forms through lessons and construction scenarios. Their presence does
not mean the solver reproduces all behavior of each form. The numerical model remains simplified and
planar, with axial member analysis and tension-only ropes.
06. Are wind and earthquakes implemented?
They are future extensions, not features we can claim as working load scenarios in the reviewed
implementation. The current demonstration focuses on self-weight and vehicle or cargo loads. Any
remaining paper wording that says otherwise should be corrected.
07. Can this replace structural design software?
No. It is a supplementary learning game. Its material capacities, stiffness assumptions and construction
representation are simplified. It does not provide the validated code checks, detailed sections or
comprehensive analyses required for professional structural design.

05 / QUESTIONS 8-14
Stress, loads, and simulation
08. How do you obtain the bridge stress percentage?
We calculate axial forces using the custom planar solver, then divide each member's force magnitude
by its applicable tension or compression capacity. Multiplying by 100 gives utilization. For example, a
6,000-newton force against a 10,000-newton capacity is 60 percent.
09. Does PhysX calculate the stress score?
The normal configured path uses the custom deterministic solver for stress feedback, scoring inputs
and capacity-based failure. PhysX handles motion, contacts and collisions. Additional deformation and
fallback behavior exists, so we describe the division of responsibilities precisely.
10. What is the difference between dead load and live load?
Dead load is the weight of the bridge members themselves. Live load is the vehicle or carried cargo
involved in the test. The solver combines the loads supported by the structure and considers the live
load's position along the road.
11. How does the solver distribute the load?
It uses member geometry, connectivity, supports and simplified stiffness to solve the equilibrium system
K u = F. It then calculates each member's axial force. This is more specific than assigning the same
force to every member or using only total vehicle weight.
12. How do you determine material strength?
Each material asset stores approximate tensile and compressive force capacities, mass per metre and
cost. The game compares the calculated member force with the relevant configured capacity. Those
values are simplified game parameters; their presence alone does not establish real-world calibration.
13. What causes failure, and what do the colors mean?
Colors indicate increasing tracked demand or warning level. A member reaching its configured axial
capacity can break under normal test rules. Excessive deformation, impacts and contract limits can also
affect failure. A successful-looking bridge still needs to satisfy the full crossing conditions.
14. Is your safety ratio a real engineering factor of safety?
It is an estimated gameplay ratio: estimated bridge capacity divided by the specified live-load mass. We
label it as an estimate and hide it for multiple-vehicle tests. It is not an independently validated safety
certification or a substitute for engineering design checks.

06 / QUESTIONS 15-21
Purpose and educational value
15. What problem does your project address?
The project addresses difficulty visualizing structural response and connecting classroom concepts with
design decisions. It provides an interactive bridge-building environment where students can change a
design, observe feedback and relate the outcome to the concepts introduced in lessons.
16. What is the main objective and who benefits?
The main objective is to develop a mobile learning-support tool for basic structural mechanics through
interactive bridge construction. The intended learners are civil engineering students at TUP Cavite.
Educators can use it for supplementary demonstrations and guided discussion.
17. What distinguishes it from entertainment bridge games?
Our project combines an intended civil-engineering learning context with NPC-guided lessons, an
Almanac, material references, quizzes and feedback linked to construction tasks. Its contribution is this
educational design and implementation; we should not claim that bridge-building gameplay itself is new.
18. How does repeated failure support learning?
Failure supplies feedback about a particular design attempt. Players can inspect the result, change
support or material choices, and compare the next test. The educational opportunity comes from
interpreting that feedback and reviewing related concepts, rather than from repeated failure alone.
19. Why include an Almanac and quizzes?
The Almanac makes discovered concepts, material references and bridge records available for review.
Quizzes provide questions and submitted-answer feedback. Together, these features create a more
explicit connection between the construction activity and the engineering concepts being introduced.
20. Why allow quiz progression at any score?
The current assessment design provides a score and answer review, then permits progression after
submission. It is not a mastery gate. This keeps the story accessible, while leaving room for future
remediation or mastery requirements if the team chooses to implement and evaluate them.
21. Can you prove the game improves learning?
The strength of that claim depends on our evidence. User ratings can show perceived usefulness,
usability and engagement. Demonstrating learning gains requires an appropriate learning assessment,
such as aligned pre/post measures. We should report only what our actual study design and verified
data support.

Methodology, evaluation, and evidence
22. Why did you use Agile development?
Iterative development let us refine mechanics, interfaces and learning flows in manageable cycles. We
could test a feature, identify problems, and revise the implementation. During the defense, we should
support that explanation with our actual sprint records, versions and testing evidence.
23. Who evaluated the system?
The current paper describes 100 participants: 90 civil engineering students and two expert groups of
five each. Before presenting, verify the actual recruitment records, institution, expert labels and
completed response counts. Explain student and expert roles separately where they answer different
evaluation questions.
24. What evaluation criteria did you use?
We must name the criteria in the questionnaire actually administered and map them consistently to our
objectives and results. The current paper names eight criteria in some sections but reports seven
differently named dimensions elsewhere. That mismatch must be reconciled before memorizing a
definitive list.
25. What does accuracy mean in your evaluation?
The revised paper describes perceived conceptual correctness, rather than independently
benchmarked numerical accuracy. Student opinions can assess whether feedback seems
understandable and appropriate. Numerical accuracy needs separate comparisons with known
solutions, and domain-expert review can assess the educational explanation.
26. What are your overall rating and statistical results?
Use only the final figures verified against the raw responses. The current draft contains conflicting
summary and table values, so this guide does not treat them as confirmed results. Prepare a corrected
result sheet showing sample size, scale, means, standard deviations and interpretations.
27. How would you validate the structural calculations?
A suitable validation plan would compare simple supported structures with hand calculations, check
self-weight and load placement, verify tension-only rope behavior, and compare repeat runs. Then test
gameplay failures and multiple loads. Identify completed checks separately from this proposed
validation plan.
28. What testing evidence can you present?
We can present the implementation checks, scenario tests and device records actually completed. A
successful compilation or isolated test is not proof of a full Android playthrough or two-device session.
We should identify the build, device, scenario, expected result and observed result for each claim.

08 / QUESTIONS 29-35
Accounts, multiplayer, and the website
29. What does PlayFab do, and what does Photon do?
PlayFab supports player authentication, cloud records and leaderboard data. Photon Fusion handles
real-time room creation, joining and networked sessions. The website adds account-facing pages and
related services. We should avoid assigning every online function to a single provider.
30. Can the game be played offline?
Guest story progress and learning activities can use local storage. Online sessions, cloud
synchronization, leaderboards and account-wallet operations require connectivity where applicable. We
should demonstrate the exact offline behavior of the prepared build rather than promise that every
account feature works offline.
31. How is the multiplayer competition scored?
A design must complete the crossing within budget to qualify. Qualified designs receive a combined
score using 40 percent cost efficiency and 60 percent strength, based on peak stress. Equal displayed
scores produce a draw. If neither qualifies, there is no winner and both outcomes are losses.
32. Why use 40 percent cost and 60 percent strength?
These are the configured scoring weights, placing more emphasis on structural performance while still
rewarding economical choices. Unless we have a documented study supporting the weights, we should
describe them as a game-design decision rather than an experimentally proven optimum.
33. How do you keep the competition comparable?
Both submitted designs use the same selected task and a frozen snapshot of load requirements. The
host controls the test and completion process. Designs must satisfy the same qualification conditions.
This supports consistency, but it is not a claim that the entire system is cheat-proof.
34. Why does the project need a website?
The website provides game information, downloads, selected leaderboards and account-facing records
such as available bridge and achievement data. Administrative tools support content and operational
management. It extends access to records and information beyond the game rather than performing the
bridge simulation itself.
35. How do purchases, rewards and cosmetics fit the project?
Rewards support progression, and cosmetics allow personal expression. Account purchases and wallet
balances use the implemented online services, while guests have local earning and spending behavior.
For the demo, identify the actual test-payment environment and avoid claiming live commercial
readiness without deployment evidence.

09 / QUESTIONS 36-42
Limits, risks, and follow-up questions
36. Why Android, and what devices are supported?
Android is the target platform within our project scope. Supported-device claims must follow measured
compatibility and performance tests. We have A12 profiling documentation, while the paper also names
an A54. Confirm which devices were actually tested and distinguish them from minimum requirements.
37. Is the game fast enough for students' phones?
We should answer with measured results for the demonstrated build and device, including gameplay,
construction and simulation. Earlier profiling does not establish current performance after later changes.
We should disclose any remaining performance limits instead of generalizing from one device.
38. How do you protect player data?
The system separates accounts and uses authenticated services for protected records. Specific
safeguards should be supported by implementation evidence and testing. Account login alone does not
prove complete security, and we should not claim compliance or comprehensive security certification
that we have not established.
39. Are all regions and features finished?
Main progression and regional contract content are authored, but the project checklist still identifies
playthroughs, balancing, temporary vehicles and ending presentation work. We will distinguish
implemented content from tested completion and demonstrate only scenarios rehearsed on the
presentation build.
40. What are the main limitations?
The structural model is simplified and planar; width is predefined; material properties are gamified; full
3D bending, shear and torsion analysis is outside the current solver. Wind, seismic and long-term
deterioration scenarios are outside the reviewed implementation. Learning claims also depend on the
evaluation evidence.
41. What would you improve next?
Priorities are to reconcile evaluation results, complete device and end-to-end testing, balance late
contracts, and improve instructional feedback. Larger extensions could include editable width, a richer
structural model, additional load scenarios and stronger learning-outcome evaluation.
42. What if the panel finds a mismatch or the demo fails?
We should acknowledge the specific issue, explain what the evidence currently supports, and identify
the correction needed. If a live scenario fails, state what happened and use a clearly labeled prepared
recording where appropriate. Do not present a recording as a successful live test.
